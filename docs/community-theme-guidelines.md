# Community theme guidelines (DRAFT, not public)

Naming (owner, 2026-11-26): always say "Theme Builder", never "design studio". Only teachers use it for now; students may get access in mid-2027 if teachers ask.

Status: draft written by the C-suite and the Design Chair, 2026-11-26, for the
owner to approve. Nothing here is public until she says publish, and the
attorney reads the removal and strike terms before they go in the Terms of Use.
Lines marked **(to confirm)** are proposals, not her decisions.

Her decisions (2026-11-26): only teachers publish (students have no accounts and
never use the Theme Builder on their own); no remixing inside Gamify; credit is
optional and is only the teacher's username; every theme is reviewed before it
goes public; a 3-strike system can delete themes and revoke theme-making, on
paid accounts too; free accounts can use every community theme; community
themes are never sold.

## License (owner, 2026-11-26: "Themes can only be inside Gamify")
Community themes use Gamify's own Community Theme License, not Creative
Commons: the teacher keeps ownership and gives Gamify and every Gamify user a
free right to use the theme unchanged, inside Gamify only. Nobody may sell it
or use it outside Gamify, and Gamify may remove it (for example after a
strike). The attorney writes the exact wording.

## What gets approved
1. **Yours to share.** Art you, your class (with permission) or Gamify made, or
   art licensed for sharing in a public library. You confirm this when you publish.
2. **Right for the youngest player.** A kindergartner could see it in class.
3. **Reads at a glance.** Pieces, board and buttons are easy to tell apart, and
   text keeps good contrast (WCAG 2.1 AA).
4. **Safe to look at.** Nothing flashes more than 3 times a second (WCAG 2.3.1).
5. **Honest name and description.** It says what the theme is.

## What gets a theme removed
- A student's full name, face, photo, voice, school ID, or any contact details.
- Characters, logos, brands, or the look of another game, show or company.
- Violence or gore, weapons aimed at people, scary content for young children.
- Sexual content, hate, slurs, bullying, or content that mocks any group.
- Drugs, alcohol, tobacco or vaping, gambling.
- Ads, links, prices, or anything that sells something.
- Political campaign or advocacy messages **(to confirm)**.
- Copies or near-copies of a theme already in the library (spam).

## Strikes (to confirm)
| Strike | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Theme removed; an email says which rule and how to fix it |
| 2 | Theme removed; publishing paused for 30 days |
| 3 | Theme-making revoked (builder and publishing), on every plan |

- **No strikes needed:** sexual content, hate, or a child's personal details
  revoke theme-making at once.
- **Strikes expire** after 12 months with no new strike.
- **One appeal** per strike, answered by the owner within 5 school days.
- **Open for the owner:** a paid teacher at strike 3 keeps the paid plan and
  only loses theme-making, or the account closes (refund question for the CFO
  and the attorney).

## Three lanes (owner, 2026-11-26: "theme reviewer should have standard for auto strikes, the rest should be pushed to me for review if flagged. also we will have rules for good to push automatically")

The lanes are hers; the lists in each lane are proposals **(to confirm)**.

| Lane | What happens | Proposed rules |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Auto-approve | Published at once, no owner review | Every piece passes the contrast and flashing checks; built only from Gamify's approved art, or the teacher ticked the rights and permission box; no words other than the theme name and description, or the words pass the word filter; not a near-copy of a library theme; teacher has no strike in the last 12 months |
| Auto-strike | Removed with a strike, email sent, no owner review | A clear match: a photo of a real child's face; a full name or contact details; a word on the blocked-words list; a near-copy of another theme; a known brand logo or character the image check recognizes |
| Flag for the owner | Held; the owner decides | Anything else the Reviewer is unsure of: possible copyrighted art, scary or violent content, borderline words, a first strike for a theme with many student contributors, every appeal |

A teacher can appeal an auto-strike once; the appeal goes to the owner.
