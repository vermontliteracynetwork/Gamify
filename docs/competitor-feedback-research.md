## Takeaways for Gamify

- **Fair fun is still the biggest gap, now on verified pages for five sites.** Power-ups change scores so "a team can answer many questions correctly and still lose", students "complain about the fairness", sabotage aims at other players, a wrong answer "will literally cost you", game skill beats accuracy, and speed and timers stress kids (one meltdown). Keep steal-style power-ups off and right answers deciding the game. Caution: the two lines the edge list quotes ("can lead to tears", "brings out the worst") are no longer verified; use the verified lines above instead.
- **Every child answers, the teacher sees it.** One-screen team play hides what each child knows and lets "one confident student" answer for the team; Flippity has "no way to remotely observe"; deeper reports are paid on two sites. Note the challenge: one rival already shows "top missed questions with one click", so a live per-question view is table stakes, not unique. Lead with it being free and working on one projected screen too.
- **One screen and own devices, both.** Teachers love no-download, no-login team play; Gamify can pair it with per-student data.
- **Trust sells, and the evidence got stronger.** Upgrade prompts "flooding the screen", parents pushed to pay, ads, inappropriate player names, under-13 email prompts, sheets that are "technically" public, answer keys findable by students. Gamify's no-upsell student pages, class-list names and no student email are direct answers.
- **Cancelling: half the edge.** Wordwall locks editing after cancel (verified); Baamboozle keeps games and folders (vendor). So "keep editing after you cancel" beats some sites, not all; never claim it until billing ships.
- **Fast setup matters more than a fresh look.** Teachers want quick builds and imports (typing everything by hand is a complaint). The "old school" look line is dropped, and one teacher found visual styles made "no significant effect" for teens: sell the learning and fairness first, the look second (Design Chair: novelty helps younger kids; test it before leaning on it).
