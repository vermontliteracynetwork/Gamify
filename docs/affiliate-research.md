## 2. What is typical

1. **Commission.** For education subscriptions the published range is about
   20 to 25% (AhaSlides 25%, SchoolhouseTeachers 20-25%, Outschool up to 20%,
   CTCMath 20%); the big kids' brands on networks pay less or a flat fee
   (IXL 7-17%, ABCmouse $3-9, Quizlet $5). Creator SaaS pays more and longer
   (Teachable 30% for a year, Thinkific 30% lifetime, Kit 50% for 12 months)
   because their customers pay $30 to $150+ a month. Year-one recurring is the
   most common shape; lifetime is rarer and is being cut back (Kajabi, Kit).
2. **Window.** 30 days is the default (Awin's stated standard; Outschool,
   Skillshare, Teachable, AhaSlides). Longer windows exist (Thinkific 90,
   Notion 180). Last click wins (AhaSlides terms; Thinkific per directory).
3. **Payout.** Monthly, by PayPal or a network, with a minimum of $25 to $100
   ($50 is the most common figure) and a hold of about 30 days for refunds
   (Thinkific). Commissions stop or are clawed back when the customer cancels
   or refunds (Teach 'n Go stops payments on cancellation).
4. **Follower discount.** Rarely published in affiliate terms; brands that
   use codes run them as separate promotions (Outschool's outreach codes).
   On TikTok a spoken code matters more than a link, so Gamify's code is a
   real edge, not a norm we are matching.
5. **Bans.** Paid search on the brand name (Canva, Kahoot), paid social ads
   (Kahoot), self-referral and referring your own employer (Teach 'n Go). The
   kids' brands sit on coupon and cashback sites (ABCmouse), which is the
   pattern Gamify bans.
6. **Platform.** Big brands use networks (Impact, Awin, CJ); creator SaaS
   uses PartnerStack; small SaaS uses Stripe-based tools (Rewardful at Teach
   'n Go, Reditus at AhaSlides). Networks track with third-party cookies.
7. **Educators.** The classroom quiz leader pays educators in product
   (free paid-plan time), not cash. No program found says anything about
   children's data or student pages; Gamify's rule is stricter than all of
   them.
8. **Who brings sales.** From memory, not from a page opened today: in most
   programs a small share of affiliates bring most sales. Plan for it and
   measure it per code.

## 4. Open questions for the owner

1. **Money or trust first?** 25% for a year is fair against education tools,
   but our dollars per sale ($17 to $36) are small next to creator SaaS. Do you
   want to compete on pay (raise to 30%), or on "teachers who use it" and keep
   25% with a later top-partner step? (Recommendation: keep 25%.)
2. **Own-school rule.** Do you agree an affiliate earns nothing when their own
   school or district buys? (Recommendation: yes; ask the attorney about
   state ethics rules for public employees.)
3. **What counts as "active"** for free Pro: one disclosed post in 60 days, or
   one paid customer a quarter?
4. **Payout method:** PayPal, bank transfer, or both? (A paid tracking tool
   later is a money decision.)
5. **Paid ads:** never, or allowed with written OK for proven partners?

For the attorney: the affiliate agreement (clawback, hold tied to the refund
policy, non-affiliation, ending a partner, disclosure duty, the free Pro as
compensation), and whether a public-school employee may take commissions.
For the accountant: W-9 timing, the 2026 1099-NEC threshold, whether PayPal
payments are reported by PayPal instead (1099-K), and how free Pro is booked.
