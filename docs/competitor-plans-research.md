# Free vs Paid: How Similar Platforms Split Their Plans

Claudia's research brief, 2026-10-24. Cross-compares the free and paid
plans of the classroom game platforms closest to Gamify, including how
they handle collectible rewards (like Blooket's Blooks), so Gamify's tiers
(`docs/product-plan.md` section 5) and XP shop (section 4F) are set with
eyes open.

> **Read this first.** Most competitors do not publish full plan details,
> and prices change often. Figures below come from the companies' own
> pages where available and otherwise from 2026 third-party reviews, which
> sometimes disagree. Where sources conflict, both numbers are shown.
> Re-check each company's live pricing page before setting final prices.
> Company names appear here only for internal research (product plan
> section 6.2); never in Gamify's customer-facing copy without a lawyer's
> okay.

## 2. Patterns across the market

1. **About $60 a year is the going rate** for an individual teacher's paid
   plan (Blooket, Gimkit, Baamboozle, Wordwall Standard, Educaplay
   Advanced, Prodigy all land between about $57 and $60). Higher tiers sit
   around $85 to $145.
2. **What competitors usually charge for:**
   - more players per live game, or more teams
   - more game modes or templates
   - homework / self-paced assignments
   - deeper reports and LMS grade sync
   - image and audio uploads, AI question generation
3. **What stays free almost everywhere:** a usable live game for a small
   class, basic question creation, and enough game modes to try it
   weekly. Free tiers that feel too tight (Wordwall's 3 activities,
   Kahoot!'s small player cap) are the ones teachers complain about most.
4. **Collectibles drive student demand.** Blooket's Blooks are a big
   reason students ask teachers to play it. Notably, Blooket earns them
   **by playing, on free accounts too**; Plus sells teacher tools, not
   student rewards.
5. **One-screen team games** (Baamboozle) charge for more teams and more
   modes, not for devices, since no student devices are involved.

## 3. The cautionary tale: paid-only rewards shown to kids

In 2021 a coalition of children's advocacy groups led by the Campaign for a
Commercial-Free Childhood (now Fairplay) filed an FTC complaint against
Prodigy, calling it a "bait-and-switch": the game is promoted as free for
schools while showing children frequent prompts for a paid membership,
with many of the most desirable items paid-only. Senator Markey and
Representative Castor asked the FTC to investigate; critics described
classrooms where "members sail around on a cloud, while non-members
literally tromp in the dirt."

> **Update 2026-10-24:** XP, the shop and Pals were removed from Gamify for now (teacher decision). The lesson below still applies to the locked game covers and to anything like it later.

**Lesson for Gamify's paid-only XP:** the risk isn't that XP is paid, it's
*children seeing what they're missing*. Gamify's design (section 4 below
and product plan 4F):
- XP belongs to the **teacher's** plan, never sold to students or
  families.
- In a free classroom, students **see no XP, no shop and no upgrade
  prompts.** One deliberate exception (teacher decision, 2026-10-24):
  paid games appear in the student Arcade with a lock on the cover, so a
  student wanting a game starts a conversation with their teacher. To
  stay clear of what drew the complaint, locked covers carry no prices,
  no "upgrade" or "buy" words, and nothing aimed at families paying.
- Within one classroom, every student has the same access: no student is
  ever a "have-not" next to a classmate.
- XP buys cosmetics only (looks, not advantages): no pay-to-win.

## 4. How Gamify's tiers compare (proposed, see product plan section 5)

| | Gamify Free | Gamify Plus (mid) | Gamify Pro (top) | Typical competitor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Classrooms | 1 | 3 | Unlimited | Usually not limited |
| Students | Up to 30 in the classroom | Proposed 40 per classroom | Proposed 60+ per classroom | Free live caps run 10 to 60 |
| Own question sets saved | 3, plus every public set | Unlimited | Unlimited | Wordwall 3; Wayground 20; others unlimited |
| Games | Free games only | All games | All games | Free tiers usually get several modes |
| Themes | Preview only (not playable) | Full library, playable | Full library + **Theme Builder** | Rare in competitors: a Gamify differentiator |
| XP and shop | **No** (and invisible to students) | Yes | Yes | Blooket gives collectibles free |
| Live monitor + Lock | No | Yes | Yes | Usually a paid or school feature |
| Accessibility settings, Share Mirror | **Free** | Free | Free | Rarely this complete anywhere |
| Price (proposed) | $0 | About $5/mo billed yearly ($60/yr) | About $9/mo billed yearly ($108/yr) | $57 to $60 typical; $85 to $145 top tiers |

### Where Gamify's plan is stronger than the market
- Share mode with **up to 10 teams free** beats one-screen competitors'
  free team caps (Baamboozle reportedly 4 free, 8 paid).
- **Accessibility and Share Mirror free**: nothing comparable in the
  free tiers above.
- A theme builder and printable theme template for every game: no competitor offers this.
- Reports on every game, modeled on a real teacher's data workflow.

### Where Gamify's plan is weaker (decisions for you)
- **Free classroom size: decided at 30 students** (2026-10-24). It
  was 20, which would have shut out many full classes. 30 covers most
  classes, though it's still below Blooket's 60-player free live games.
- **XP paid-only** while Blooket gives collectibles free: students in free
  classes may prefer the competitor. The upside is real: student demand
  for XP pushes teachers to upgrade, which is why it's a strong paid
  feature, as long as section 3's rules hold.
- **3 saved question sets** matches Wordwall's tight cap, which teachers
  complain about. Public sets help a lot here; a good public library is
  essential at launch.
